## 3. Data Cleaning & Row-Count Flow Analysis

### Empirical Row-Count Flow

| Step | Operation / Filter | Rows Remaining | Rows Dropped | Percentage of Initial | Methodological Rationale & Validity |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **0** | Raw Survey File (`h251.dta`) | 18,919 | 0 | 100.00% | Full 2023 consolidated survey cohort. |
| **1** | `AGE23X >= 0` | 18,771 | 148 | 99.22% | **Valid:** Drops individuals ineligible/out-of-scope during round (`-1 = INAPPLICABLE`). |

### Impact of Excluding Zero-Expenditure Records

1. **Sample Selection Bias:** In the civilian noninstitutionalized population, **13.91% of individuals incurred zero medical costs in 2023**. These are primarily healthy young individuals. Discarding them truncates the dependent variable distribution.
2. **Inability to Predict Zero:** A model trained exclusively on $Y > 0$ with `log1p` will always predict a strictly positive expense for every living individual, overpredicting costs for healthy populations.
3. **Health Economics Standards:** Standard econometrics (Manning et al., 1987; Duan et al., 1983) dictates either:
   - **Two-Part Models (2PM):** Part 1 estimates $\Pr(Y > 0)$, Part 2 estimates $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid Y > 0]$.
   - **Tweedie Compound Poisson-Gamma GLM:** Directly accommodates the point mass at $Y = 0$ and the skewed continuous positive tail.

### Retransformation & Log-Scale Distortion

In [`ml_model/train_model.py:103`](file:///Users/arpitdoshi/Desktop/ipd/HealthScore/ml_model/train_model.py#L103), the target is transformed via $y_{\log} = \ln(1 + \text{TOTEXP23})$.
At evaluation:
```python
y_pred_test = xgb_regressor.predict(X_test)
r2 = r2_score(y_test_log, y_pred_test) # Calculated on LOG scale
mae = mean_absolute_error(np.expm1(y_test_log), np.expm1(y_pred_test)) # Dollar scale
```

**Key Findings:**
1. **$R^2$ Metric Ambiguity:** The reported $R^2 \approx 0.25$ is the $R^2$ of log expenditure, **not dollar expenditure**. When dollar predictions are evaluated, naive $\text{expm1}$ retransformation results in a dollar $R^2 \approx 0.005$ to $0.012$ due to extreme right-tail skewness!
2. **Jensen's Inequality:** Since $\mathbb{E}[\exp(Y)] \ge \exp(\mathbb{E}[Y])$, back-transforming conditional mean log predictions underestimates expected dollar spending unless adjusted by a smearing factor (Duan, 1983) or lognormal variance adjustment ($\exp(\hat{\mu} + \sigma^2/2)$).
3. **Duan Smear Limitation:** While Duan smearing works for strictly positive data ($Y > 0$), applying global smearing to $\ln(1+Y)$ when 14% of values are zero over-inflates predictions for low-risk spenders.

### 5.2. Clarification on "ACG" Morbidity Clusters: Custom Heuristics vs. Proprietary Grouper Software

The codebase names its six clinical aggregation features with an `_ACG` suffix:
- `Cardio_ACG`: `HIBPDX + CHDDX + MIDX + STRKDX`
- `Metabolic_ACG`: `DIABDX_M18 + CHOLDX`
- `Respiratory_ACG`: `ASTHDX + EMPHDX + CHBRON31`
- `Oncology_ACG`: `CANCERDX`
- `Musculoskeletal_ACG`: `ARTHDX + JTPAIN31_M18`
- `Psychiatric_ACG`: `ADDPRS42 + ADHDADDX`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Methodological Clarification for Research Reporting:**  
> These six morbidity clusters are **custom heuristic condition counts** constructed from 13 self-reported MEPS survey indicators. **They were NOT produced using the official Johns Hopkins ACG® (Adjusted Clinical Groups) System software.**  
> The official Johns Hopkins ACG grouper is a proprietary clinical risk-adjustment software suite that processes individual-level medical claims, comprehensive ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes, NDC pharmacy data, and encounter types to compute Aggregated Diagnosis Groups (ADGs), Expanded Diagnosis Clusters (EDCs), and Resource Utilization Bands (RUBs).  
> In the research paper and documentation, these variables must be described transparently as *"heuristic chronic disease clusters inspired by multi-system morbidity categorizations"* rather than claiming direct Johns Hopkins ACG software outputs.

### 5.3. Composite Clinical Indicators

1. **`Comorbidity_Count`:** Integer sum of affected disease clusters (0 to 6).
2. **`Multimorbidity_Penalty`:** $\text{Count}^2$. Represents the non-linear, compounding healthcare utilization observed in patients with multi-organ chronic conditions.
3. **`Frailty_Index`:** Proposed heuristic composite:
   $$\text{Frailty} = \text{ADGENH42} + 2 \times \text{ANYLMI23} + 2 \times \text{DFCOG42}$$
   Range: 1 (Excellent health, no limitation, no cognitive impairment) to 9 (Poor health + physical limitations + cognitive impairment).
4. **Interaction Terms:** `Age_x_Frailty` and `Age_x_Comorbidity` capture the synergistic compounding effect of age with functional impairment and disease burden.

## 6. Training vs. Production Inference Discrepancy & Parity Analysis

A major audit finding in the original codebase was the severe disconnect between features used during training and features available at inference:

### 6.1. Historical Defect (Original Codebase Prior to Audit)
- **Silent Imputation to Zero:** Training used 14 features including adult survey variables (`K6SUM42` psychological distress, `ADGENH42` perceived health, `ANYLMI23` limitation, and `DFCOG42` cognitive difficulty). At inference time, `risk_engine.py` hardcoded `'K6SUM42': 0` and defaulted perceived health to 3.
- **Conflating Missing Data with Negative Health Status:** Treating an unasked or uncollected survey item as zero meant that all patients were silently evaluated as having zero psychological distress and zero functional limitations, skewing predictions downward and distorting risk percentiles.
- **Unused Frontend Inputs:** Frontend intake collects patient `height` and `weight`, but these were completely ignored because MEPS HC-251 does not include BMI variables.

### 6.2. Current Implementation & Parity Resolution
To resolve this gap methodologically without breaking frontend integration:
1. **Observable-Inputs Primary Candidate for Integration Testing:** An 11-feature model trained strictly on directly observable demographics and condition clusters (`AGE23X`, `Gender_Male`, 6 disease clusters, `Comorbidity_Count`, `Multimorbidity_Penalty`, `Age_x_Comorbidity`) was implemented and designated as the leading candidate for frontend integration testing. For ordinal expenditure ranking, Observable XGBoost achieves test MAE of **$7,516.26** compared to **$7,495.29** for the 14-feature deployment-imputed model (a negligible delta of $20.97 / 0.28%), while for un-logged dollar prediction, Observable Tweedie GLM achieves **$8,987.94** (vs. **$8,972.22** for 14-feature imputed Tweedie), completely eliminating the need to synthesize unobserved SAQ variables at runtime.
2. **Subgroup Parity Verification:** In pediatric ($2,929.76 vs $2,942.54) and senior ($12,543.62 vs $12,549.69) cohorts, the 11-feature observable model actually achieves lower test MAE than the imputed 14-feature model.
3. **Empirical Conditional Imputation Table:** For callers utilizing the 14-feature benchmark, conditional expectations learned directly from the training cohort ($N=15,016$) are available. Special MEPS non-response codes (`-7 REFUSED`, `-8 DON'T KNOW`, `-9 NOT ASCERTAINED`) were explicitly filtered out, and pediatric skip patterns (`-1 INAPPLICABLE`) are handled via empirical pediatric baselines (`ADGENH42 = 2.0`, `K6SUM42 = 0.0`), preventing unasked children from inheriting adult illness means.
4. **Explicit Diagnostic Return:** The inference engine returns `data_parity_diagnostics` listing which features were directly observed vs. imputed, ensuring total transparency.

### 6.3. Unresolved Architecture Gap (Frontend Intake vs Clinical Depth)
While the 11-feature observable model resolves deployment parity cleanly, capturing deeper individual health state (rather than population conditional means) would require expanding the frontend intake questionnaire to collect:
- Self-rated general health (VR-12 single item: Excellent, Very Good, Good, Fair, Poor).
- Functional limitations and cognitive difficulties.
- Kessler-6 psychological distress screener.
Until these questions are added to the frontend patient portal, the 11-feature observable engine remains the statistically defensible candidate for integration testing.

### 7.2. Statistically Defensible Task Definition:
To evaluate a companion classifier with rigorous methodology:
- **Target Definition:** **Top-decile annual healthcare expenditure** ($\text{TOTEXP23} \ge \text{P90}$).
- **High Expenditure vs. Clinical/Catastrophic Risk:** This classification task identifies individuals in the upper 10th percentile of annual health spending. It must be defined strictly as **high expenditure**, rather than conflated with clinical morbidity or catastrophic clinical risk.
- **Strict In-Sample Threshold Derivation:**
  - The threshold must be derived **solely from the training split** ($N = 15,016$) to eliminate test-set leakage.
  - **Unweighted Training Threshold (Target 1):** $\text{TOTEXP23} \ge \mathbf{\$20,360.50}$ (Train prevalence: $10.00\%$; held-out test prevalence: $10.28\%$; Target-specific baseline Brier = **$0.0922$**).

This claim is unsupported and has been removed; CMS-HCC commercial risk adjustment reinsurance attachment points operate under statutory levels (e.g., $45,000–$100,000+), which are unrelated to MEPS sample percentiles.
- **Contemporaneous vs. Prospective Prediction:** All survey features (conditions, distress, limitations) and the expenditure target (`TOTEXP23`) are measured over calendar year 2023. **Same-year features predicting same-year expenditure establish contemporaneous cross-sectional association, NOT prospective next-year risk prediction.** Longitudinal prospective validation requires multi-year panel files (e.g., MEPS HC-257 Panel 28 Longitudinal File: 2023 predictors $\to$ 2024 outcomes), while contemporaneous external validation requires MEPS HC-256 (2024 Full Year Consolidated File).

## 8. Target Population, Survey Weights & Complex Sampling Design

### 8.1. Target Population & Sampling Structure
- **Target Population:** The **civilian noninstitutionalized population of the United States** during calendar year 2023.
- **Sampling Design:** MEPS uses a complex multistage stratified cluster sampling design drawn from the National Health Interview Survey (NHIS) sampling frame.
- **Survey Design Variables in HC-251:**
  - `PERWT23F`: Final person-level sampling weight (accounts for selection probability, non-response, and post-stratification to U.S. Census population totals).
  - `VARSTR`: Stratum indicator for Taylor series variance estimation.
  - `VARPSU`: Primary Sampling Unit (PSU) cluster indicator.

### 8.2. Survey-Weighted Fitting Audit & Weight Normalization
- **Weight Scale Impact:** Person weights sum to $\sim 265,586,855$ with a mean of $\sim 17,687$. Passing unscaled weights into standard ML solvers heavily distorts loss-to-regularization balance (e.g. un-regularizing $C=1.0$ in `LogisticRegression` and inflating Hessian sums in XGBoost).
- **Weight Normalization:** Weights used for ML solvers were normalized ($w_{\text{norm}} = w \times \frac{N}{\sum w}$, $\text{mean}=1.0$, preserving effective sample size $N=15,016$).
- **Complex Survey Inference Limits:** Incorporating normalized person weights addresses unequal selection probabilities and non-response, but does **not** constitute full complex-survey inference. Design variables (`VARSTR`, `VARPSU`) and Taylor series linearization / replicate weights needed for design-based standard errors were not applied.

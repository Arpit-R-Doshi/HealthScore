## 1. Why Was Precision Low ($23.18\%$) at Threshold $\tau = 0.10$?

Our six-factor empirical diagnostic investigation ([`THRESHOLD_SENSITIVITY_REPORT.md`](file:///Users/arpitdoshi/Desktop/ipd/HealthScore/research_completion/crc_revision/THRESHOLD_SENSITIVITY_REPORT.md) and Section 6.5 of [`latex/final_crc_paper.pdf`](file:///Users/arpitdoshi/Desktop/ipd/HealthScore/research_completion/crc_revision/latex/final_crc_paper.pdf)) explains the $23.18\%$ precision at $\tau = 0.10$ through four interacting mathematical and domain mechanisms:
1. **Theoretical Volume-Ceiling Bound (Primary Direct Cause):** Setting $\tau = 0.10$ flags $39.97\%$ of the internal holdout test cohort ($1,501 / 3,755$ patients). Because only $P = 450$ true high-expenditure patients ($\ge \$17,827.41$) exist in the entire test cohort ($11.98\%$), **even a hypothetical perfect oracle classifier ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 1.00$, $100\%$ recall) that flags $1,501$ patients is mathematically bounded by $\text{Precision}_{\text{oracle}} = 450 / 1,501 = 29.98\%$**. At the reference threshold of $\tau = 0.10$, the observed precision of $23.18\%$ remains below the $29.98\%$ theoretical upper bound imposed by the flagged cohort volume (where the ratio $23.18\% / 29.98\% = 348 / 450 = 77.33\%$ equals model recall rather than independent evidence against overfitting).
2. **Class Imbalance & Bayes Base-Rate Conditioning:** With an unweighted positive prevalence of $\pi = 11.984\%$ ($10.506\%$ survey-weighted), non-high-cost individuals outnumber high-cost individuals by $7.344 : 1$ ($8.518 : 1$ weighted). Operating at $77.33\%$ recall and $65.11\%$ specificity ($\text{FPR} = 34.89\%$) inevitably produces $3,305 \times 0.3489 = 1,153$ false positives.
3. **Well-Calibrated Intermediate Probability Concentration:** More than half ($53.90\%$, $809 / 1,501$) of all patients flagged at $\tau = 0.10$ reside in the $[0.10, 0.20)$ probability band ($449$ in $[0.10, 0.15)$ with mean $\hat{p}=12.35\%$ vs. observed $14.92\%$; $360$ in $[0.15, 0.20)$ with mean $\hat{p}=17.35\%$ vs. observed $19.44\%$). Because the model is well-calibrated (`Weighted BSS = +0.1025`, `Unweighted Brier = 0.0936`, `CITL = +0.0646`, `Slope = 0.9438`), assigning binary `1` to patients whose true event probability is $15\%\text{--}19\%$ converts the remaining $81\%\text{--}85\%$ into $672$ false positives ($58.28\%$ of all $1,153$ false positives).
4. **Cross-Cohort Generalization Comparison Across 8 Splits & Feature Granularity Ceiling:** Across all 8 internal and external evaluation splits in Table 9 (`tab:overfitting_audit`)—including 5-Fold Stratified OOF (`ROC-AUC = 0.7795`), 5-Fold Household (`DUID`) OOF (`ROC-AUC = 0.7785`), and the **strictly disjoint 2024 `HC-256` Panel 29 cohort ($N=10,037$, `ROC-AUC = 0.7824` Unw / `0.7812` Wtd, `PR-AUC = 0.3506`, `Wtd BSS = +0.1242`)**—similar discrimination provides no evidence of a substantial generalization collapse under the evaluated protocols; however, it does not establish the absence of overfitting. The remaining discrimination ceiling (`0.7654` Weighted Logistic vs. `0.7693` Random Forest and `0.7705` XGBoost) reflects the absence of clinical lab severity biomarkers and stochastic acute trauma/surgical shocks ($10.22\%$ of high-cost cases have `Comorbidity_Count = 0`).

## 2. How Does Performance Change Across Thresholds $\tau = 0.10$ to $0.90$?

Holding the fitted classifier (`risk_model_classifier_observable_weighted_p90.pkl`) strictly fixed ([`threshold_comparison_test.csv`](file:///Users/arpitdoshi/Desktop/ipd/HealthScore/research_completion/crc_revision/results/threshold_comparison_test.csv), Tables 7 & 8 `tab:threshold_operating_points` / `tab:threshold_sweep`):
- **$\tau = 0.05$ (Intermediate Ultra-Wide Cutoff):** Flags $60.72\%$ ($2,280$); Precision $= 17.59\%$ ($1.47\times$), Recall $= 89.11\%$, F1 $= 0.2938$, Specificity $= 43.15\%$, Balanced Accuracy $= 0.6613$ ($1,879\text{ FP}, 49\text{ FN}$).
- **$\tau = 0.10$ (High-Sensitivity Operating Point):** Flags $39.97\%$ ($1,501$); Precision $= 23.18\%$ ($1.93\times$; Weighted $22.54\%$), Recall $= 77.33\%$ (Weighted $71.93\%$), F1 $= 0.3567$ (Weighted $0.3433$), Specificity $= 65.11\%$, **Balanced Accuracy $= 0.7122$ (Maximum; Val OOF $= 0.7152$)** ($1,153\text{ FP}, 102\text{ FN}$). On disjoint 2024 Panel 29 ($N=10,037$): Precision $= 23.78\%$, Recall $= 78.46\%$, F1 $= 0.3650$, Balanced Accuracy $= 0.7121$.
- **$\tau = 0.15$ (Fine-Grid F1 Peak):** Flags $28.02\%$ ($1,052$); Precision $= 26.71\%$ ($2.23\times$), Recall $= 62.44\%$, **Unweighted Holdout F1 $= 0.3742$ (Weighted F1 $= 0.3525$; Val OOF F1 $= 0.3796$)**, Specificity $= 76.67\%$, Balanced Accuracy $= 0.6956$ ($771\text{ FP}, 169\text{ FN}$). On disjoint 2024 Panel 29: Precision $= 28.65\%$, Recall $= 66.14\%$, **F1 $= 0.3998$**.
- **$\tau = 0.20$ (Primary-Grid F1-Optimal Operating Point):** Flags $18.43\%$ ($692$); **Precision $= 30.49\%$ ($2.54\times$; Weighted $30.73\%$)**, Recall $= 46.89\%$ (Val OOF $50.23\%$), **Holdout F1 $= 0.3695$ (Primary-Grid Peak; Val OOF F1 $= 0.3855$, Val Wtd F1 $= 0.3551$)**, Specificity $= 85.45\%$, Balanced Accuracy $= 0.6617$ ($481\text{ FP}$ — **a $58.28\%$ reduction in false positives** vs. $\tau=0.10$, $239\text{ FN}$). On disjoint 2024 Panel 29 ($N=10,037$), $\tau=0.20$ achieves **$32.69\%$ precision, $52.38\%$ recall, and $0.4026$ F1**.
- **$\tau = 0.25$ (Precision–Recall Breakeven):** Flags $11.72\%$ ($440$); Precision $= 35.00\%$ ($2.92\times$), Recall $= 34.22\%$, F1 $= 0.3461$, Specificity $= 91.35\%$ ($286\text{ FP} \approx 296\text{ FN}$). On disjoint 2024 Panel 29: Precision $= 37.77\%$, Recall $= 40.62\%$, F1 $= 0.3914$.
- **$\tau = 0.30$ (Higher-Specificity Operating Point):** Flags $7.59\%$ ($285$); **Precision $= 40.00\%$ ($3.34\times$; $42.29\%$ on 2024 Panel 29)**, Recall $= 25.33\%$ ($30.29\%$ on Panel 29), F1 $= 0.3102$ ($0.3529$ on Panel 29), **Specificity $= 94.83\%$** ($171\text{ FP}$ — **an $85.17\%$ reduction in false positives**, $336\text{ FN}$).
- **$\tau = 0.40$:** Flags $2.77\%$ ($104$); Precision $= 44.23\%$ ($3.69\times$), Recall $= 10.22\%$, F1 $= 0.1661$, Specificity $= 98.25\%$ ($58\text{ FP}, 404\text{ FN}$).
- **$\tau = 0.50$ to $0.90$ (High-Cutoff Recall Collapse):** Because calibrated probabilities are bounded by $\max(\hat{p}_{\text{test}}) = 0.8008$ ($\text{P99} = 0.4981$), $\tau = 0.50$ flags only $36$ patients ($0.96\%$, Precision $= 41.67\%$, Recall $= 3.33\%$, F1 $= 0.0617$), $\tau = 0.60$ flags $7$ (Recall $= 1.33\%$), $\tau = 0.70$ flags $2$ (Recall $= 0.44\%$), $\tau = 0.80$ flags $1$ (Recall $= 0.22\%$), and $\tau = 0.90$ flags $0$ patients (Recall $= 0.00\%$).

## 3. What Are the Representative Statistical Operating Points and Why?

Selected strictly on leak-free **5-Fold Out-of-Fold (OOF) validation ($N=15,016$)**, we present three **statistical operating points** (Section 6.5 and Table 7 `tab:threshold_operating_points`) rather than a single static cutoff or unvalidated clinical care pathways:
1. **High-Sensitivity Operating Point ($\tau = 0.10$ — Max Balanced Accuracy $= 0.7152$ OOF / $0.7122$ Holdout):** Retain as the high-sensitivity reference baseline ($77.33\%$ holdout recall [$78.46\%$ Panel 29], $23.18\%$ holdout precision [$23.78\%$ Panel 29], $1.93\times$ enrichment).
2. **Primary-Grid F1-Optimal Operating Point ($\tau = 0.20$ — Validation Primary-Grid F1 Peak $= 0.3855$ OOF):** Present as the primary balanced statistical operating point ($30.49\%$ holdout precision [$2.54\times$ enrichment; $32.69\%$ on Panel 29], $46.89\%$ holdout recall [$50.23\%$ OOF, $52.38\%$ Panel 29], $85.45\%$ specificity, $0.3695$ holdout F1 [$0.4026$ Panel 29], cutting false positives by $58.28\%$; with $\tau = 0.15$ noted as the fine-grid F1 peak at $0.3742$ Holdout / $0.3998$ Panel 29). Maximizing F1 provides a harmonic summary of precision and recall, but does not by itself establish clinical usefulness.
3. **Higher-Specificity Operating Point ($\tau = 0.30$ — Reduced Flagged-Volume Cutoff):** Present as a higher-specificity operating point ($40.00\%$ holdout precision [$3.34\times$ enrichment; $42.29\%$ on Panel 29], $94.83\%$ specificity, $25.33\%$ holdout recall [$30.29\%$ Panel 29], flagging $7.59\%$ of patients and cutting false positives by $85.17\%$).
